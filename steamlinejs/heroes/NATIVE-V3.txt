GREENHAVEN VBXE V3 — BALANCE, ENCOUNTERS AND MONSTER RULES

Build and play
  cd atari/city
  node tools/import-balance.js ../../greenhaven-unit-balance.json
  sh build.sh
Open atari/city/city-vbxe.xex with VBXE enabled. Build defaults to the improved
vbxe-v2 artwork. HEROES_ART_PROFILE=classic sh build.sh uses the original art
with the current gameplay. Node 22, ImageMagick and MADS are required.

Preservation
The exact pre-integration native executable, native config, editor and editor
config are in backups/before-native-abilities-2026-10-17/. Older backups remain.
The 150 original art inputs remain unchanged. The old art comparison and its

Picture-story introduction
Three illustrated screens play once during XEX loading: the prince and forest
nymph fall in love; the necromancer abducts her with no army in that scene;
then the furious prince leads his troops. Raise an army at the palace, defeat
the necromancer and rescue her. Fire, Enter or Space advances
each page; release the button between presses. The game starts at the palace.
The 320x200 screens use the existing shared palette and temporarily occupy
the two screen buffers and the city-background cache. Game loading replaces
the third picture with city art and overwrites ALL intro code at $7000 with
battle data. The running game reserves no RAM or VRAM for the introduction.
Source illustrations and exact image-generation prompts are in art/intro-v1/.

Integrated balance
Starts with 50 gold and two 4-HP Spearmen. The editor's current recruitment,
individual upgrade and building prices are imported. Chapel costs 40/50/30,
unlocking the fourth/fifth slots and then a 10-gold recruitment discount.
Cavalry deals 3 damage; Demon has 3 HP, from the earlier simulation candidate.

Unlimited encounters and unlocks
34 weighted enemy groups across four regions and three initial difficulty stages.
Dark Forest -> Catacombs -> Monster Caves -> Necromancer Ruins. Three victories
unlock the next lair; the current lair stays open for unlimited battles.
There is no WINS x/3 text or battle limit in the pre-battle popup.
After reaching the unlock threshold, each further victory adds one enemy HP;
every three such wins adds one base damage. Reward increases by 10 gold for each
HP step. Native caps are 99 HP and 9 base damage; Ogre preparation can add 2.
The internal victory counter saturates at 255 to avoid wrapping or reading past
the stage tables. It does not restrict entry, even after reaching that value.
Further battles remain at maximum strength once the safe stat caps are reached.
Defeat/retreat preserves the encounter seed and difficulty. The old flat Practice
option is removed. Early fights use the authored pools; harder repeat fights
use the final pool with strengthened enemies. Summons scale with the lair too.

Native randomness uses an 8-bit seed initialized from Atari RANDOM and clock.
Every successful battle advances it, wrapping after 255. Defeat and retreat
preserve it. All 256 native seeds use the exact editor's weighted 32-bit hash
and groups, via compiled tables. Editor previews also accept larger seeds;
use 0..255 for direct native comparisons. Simulation uses the native seed cycle.

Monster abilities
Vampire (existing dark ID/art): dealing damage heals 1 HP, capped at original HP.
Zombie: killing a hero converts its battlefield slot to an enemy zombie with
2 HP. It acts next round. At most two conversions per battle, and raised zombies
cannot convert more soldiers. The original soldier is permanently lost from the campaign army, including
when the battle is won.
Skeleton: Archery Range arrows deal one less damage, minimum 1. Magic/melee
remain unchanged. Bone Archers do not gain this resistance.
Ogre: instead of attacking immediately, prepares a +2 damage strike against the
victim's hex. Next enemy phase it stands still and attacks that hex if a hero
remains adjacent. Moving away avoids the hit. WIND status identifies preparation.
Imp: after hitting, hops to the first open adjacent hex farther from its victim.
Warlock: summons skeletons from round 2, at most two living summons. New summons
act next round. Dead enemy slots are recycled if the twelve allocated slots fill.
Killing the Warlock wins immediately. Ability messages appear above the board.

Verification and limitations
Tests execute the assembled 6502 code with a functional VBXE blitter at both
D600 and D700. They cover all abilities, caps, delays, seeded groups, rewards,
unlimited lairs, army purchases and upgrades, animation, UI controls and memory bounds.
This checks logic and pixels, not physical Atari raster/DMA timing or human
play difficulty. No physical-hardware run has been performed.
The editor simulator has the same special rules, with healing for survivors, purchases,
upgrades and tactical logs. Its player uses fixed shopping plans and pathfinding;
its default enemy follows the native greedy movement. Simulation is not native
execution. Actual tactical choices can differ. Returning to earlier lairs is allowed, but each victory there also increases difficulty.

Benchmark
node simulation/abilities-benchmark.cjs PATH_TO_OUTPUT_DIRECTORY
Runs 2,560 campaigns: five strategies x 256 starting seeds x abilities on/off.
The current benchmark uses permanent casualties and paid replacements.
Completion depends on strategy and starting seed; the earlier 100% result
assumed free restoration and no longer applies. See the latest generated report.
The generated HTML includes full purchase and tactical logs for five examples.
The browser editor compares abilities on/off without changing the active draft.

Double-Fire input fix (2026-10-18)
The first Fire on the already selected soldier now only arms the second press;
it does not trigger a blocking battlefield redraw. This keeps input polling
active to capture the release and rapid second Fire. Selecting another soldier
still redraws, held Fire still cannot skip, and the second press still requires
a release within the existing double-Fire interval. The last soldier's skip
runs the enemy phase and starts the next player round.
Regression: python3 atari/city/tools/test-double-fire-turn.py
The previous v3 executable/source are preserved in
backups/before-double-fire-fix-2026-10-18/.

Encounter popup availability fix (2026-10-18)
Unavailable Fight controls now show LOCKED without a button background or active
outline, and navigation skips them. Locked regions and empty armies default to
Back. The report shows the reason (previous region required or recruit an army).
Practice is selectable only in Dark Forest after a Forest victory and with an
army. Its unavailable state is informational; other regions omit it entirely.
All popup entry, navigation and confirmation use the same availability checks.
Regression: python3 atari/city/tools/test-encounter-popup.py

Damage-scaled impact animation (2026-10-18)
Attacks now use eight approach/contact/recovery frames. On contact, the target
recoils up to five pixels away from the attacker and returns to its original
position. A red point appears over its body for the impact frames: 1 HP lost
uses 1x1 pixel, 2 HP 3x3, 3 HP 5x5, and 4+ HP 7x7. Size uses actual HP lost,
including skeleton arrow resistance, the Ogre bonus and remaining HP on lethal
hits. This is a visual offset, never a change to the occupied hex. Summons do
not trigger damage effects. Ranged projectiles reach the target before impact.
Regression: python3 atari/city/tools/test-hit-impact.py

Character bars removed (2026-10-18)
Removed the flat colored bars beneath battlefield soldiers and monsters.
Health remains in the numeric bottom panel; hit recoil and red impact points
remain visible.

Permanent casualties (2026-10-18)
A soldier killed in any fight is removed immediately from its original campaign
slot, even when the battle is won. The same applies to defeats and retreats.
A Zombie conversion permanently removes the original soldier too. No gold is
refunded; its recorded purchase/upgrade investment is cleared. Empty army slots
remain empty until the player buys replacements. Surviving soldiers still heal
between fights. An empty army cannot enter battle; insufficient replacement
funds can leave the campaign lost. No automatic rescue units are granted.
The simulator uses the same rule and purchases replacements at current building
tiers and full normal recruitment prices (including any earned Chapel discount).
Regression tests:
  python3 atari/city/tools/test-permanent-casualties.py
  node tests/permanent-casualties.test.cjs
The previous preset, native config and executable are preserved in
backups/before-permanent-casualties-2026-10-18/.

Endless lair progression (2026-10-18)
The previous fixed Practice/replay descriptions above are historical and are
superseded by the unlimited encounter rules. Unlock thresholds only gate the
next lair. They never close the current lair or stop further battles.
Native regression: python3 atari/city/tools/test-endless-lairs.py
Editor/simulator regression: node tests/endless-lairs.test.cjs
The stats benchmark still has a finite target (three wins in every region) for
comparison; reaching that target does not end the actual game or editor preview.
Previous configs and executable: backups/before-endless-lairs-2026-10-18/.

Game over and restart (2026-10-18)
The campaign now enters GAME OVER when no soldiers remain and no recruit from
any currently built recruiting building is affordable. It compares the current
highest unlocked tier in each building, applies Chapel's discount, and checks
16-bit prices. Low gold alone is not a loss while soldiers remain alive.
The defeat check runs after battle actions/rewards finish and after city actions.
The game-over screen explains the loss. Fire starts a new campaign with the
normal initial gold, buildings, two Spearmen and locked lairs. A held Fire press
cannot also open the new city's popup. Escape/directions do not bypass game over.
Simulator runs now expose gameOver separately from a fixed buying strategy that
cannot afford its preferred replacement while a cheaper recruit is still possible.
Tests: python3 atari/city/tools/test-game-over.py; node tests/game-over.test.cjs
